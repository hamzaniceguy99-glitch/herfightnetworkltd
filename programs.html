What we run, in detail

Four levels, with the same head-injury rule applied to all of them.

Program 01 · No contact at all

Beginners' classes

Nobody spars in a beginners' class and nobody is pushed towards it. A lot of women arrive expecting to be thrown in, having heard that is how these places work, and then stay for months doing technique and conditioning without ever getting hit. That is a complete way to train and we treat it as one, not as a stage on the way somewhere.

  • Per class
  • Women 18+
  • Screening completed
  • No contact

Program 02 · Skill, not damage

Technique and pad work

Pads and bags let you build real skill and real conditioning without accumulating head impacts, which is why most of our training volume sits here rather than in sparring. Repeated sub-concussive contact is a genuine long-term concern in combat sport, and training that minimises it without losing the skill is simply better coaching.

  • Per class
  • Women 18+
  • Screening completed
  • Minimal contact

Program 03 · Optional, always watched

Supervised sparring

Sparring is never unsupervised, never a condition of membership, and never an open invitation to go hard. Partners are matched for size and experience, protective equipment is required, and a coach controls the intensity. You can say no to a round, or to sparring entirely, without giving a reason and without it affecting your standing here.

  • Per session
  • Experienced members
  • Coach present
  • Gear required

Program 04 · Through the governing body

Competition preparation

Competition goes through the recognised governing body for the discipline, with its medical, licensing and age requirements — we are not a licensed professional promoter and we do not stage professional contests. We also do not run rapid weight cuts or dehydration practices, and we will not enter someone whose weight management looks unsafe.

  • Per camp
  • Competing members
  • Medical required
  • No weight cutting
